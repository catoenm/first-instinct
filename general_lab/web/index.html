What we built

We started with Qwen3.5-9B, a pretrained language model with nine billion parameters. We adapted it on 350,857 examples: public instruction tasks, reasoning problems checked by code, and simulated decision scenarios.

Small trainable components throughout the language network learn to score the answers you provide. The parcel question is just one example; the underlying interface accepts your own questions and choices.

We’re also testing reinforcement learning: letting the model gather evidence, take actions, and learn from what happens. The experiment asks whether training it to forecast outcomes improves its decisions and probability estimates.
